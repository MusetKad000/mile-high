The interview method

Almost every pro cheer interview question is one of seven types. Once you name the type in your head, you know the shape of the answer, and you only need your five stories.

Done whenYou can name the type of any question and answer it in under 60 seconds.

The answer formula

01One-sentence answer
02One specific example
03Tie it to the Broncos

Aim for 30–60 seconds. Stop the moment the point is made.

The seven question types

Background
“Tell us about yourself.”

They're askingAre you well-rounded and articulate?

Answer shapeThree beats: dance, career or school, community. 45 seconds.

Motivation
“Why the Broncos? Why now?”

They're askingIs this a real commitment or a whim?

Answer shapeYour personal Denver or Broncos link, then what you'll give back.

Behavioral
“Tell us about a time…”

They're askingHow do you actually act under pressure?

Answer shapeSituation, what you did, the result, the lesson.

Situational
“What would you do if…”

They're askingCan we trust your judgment with our brand?

Answer shapeCalm, team-first, protect the brand, loop in the director.

Knowledge
“Who's our head coach?”

They're askingDid you do your homework?

Answer shapeA short, confident fact. If unsure, say so and pivot to what you do know.

Values
“What does being a role model mean?”

They're askingCharacter and maturity.

Answer shapeOne belief, backed by one real example from your life.

Curveball
“If you were a snack, what would you be?”

They're askingCan you think on your feet and have fun?

Answer shapeSmile, answer fast, tie it to a trait. “Popcorn: I'm always at the game.”

Worked examples

Use the shape, then rewrite every line in your own words.

“Tell us about yourself.”Background
I've danced for [X] years, mostly jazz and pom, and I [current job or school]. Outside of that I volunteer with [cause], which is where I found how much I love working with kids. Being a Broncos Cheerleader would let me bring all three together, on and off the field.
“Tell us about a time you got hard feedback.”Behavioral
Last year a coach told me my performance read too small for a stadium. It stung, but I started filming every rep and comparing it to game-day footage. Within two months that same coach used my video as the example. Now I ask for feedback before anyone has to give it.
“A fan says something inappropriate at an appearance.”Situational
I'd stay polite and not engage with the comment, step back toward my teammates, and let the staff on site handle it. Afterward I'd tell our director so the whole team is protected next time.
“Why do you want to be a Denver Broncos Cheerleader?”Motivation
[A specific Broncos memory or Denver connection.] The DBC aren't only performers. They're in schools and hospitals all year and run the Junior DBC clinics for kids. That community side is what I want to be part of, and I'm ready to commit to it every week.

Media training

Some NFL auditions run on-camera interviews and public-speaking stations, and DBC members do TV, radio and podcast spots. The camera is part of the job.

Done whenThree filmed mock interviews, each reviewed on mute and audio-only.

On-camera rules

Eyes on the interviewer

Not the lens, unless the crew asks.

Repeat the question

“My favorite Broncos moment is…” so the clip works on its own.

Smile at both ends

At the start and finish of every answer.

Slow down 20%

Nerves speed everyone up.

Never go negative

About another team, player or candidate.

Hands quiet

Relaxed, and no hair touching mid-answer.

The look

The headshot is the first impression and the audition look is the lasting one. Lock both in by February so March is only about performing.

Done whenHeadshots are back and you've danced a full combo in the complete look.
Outfit

Most NFL teams ask for a two-piece: sports-bra top and dance shorts. Choose a color that flatters your skin tone; rhinestones add polish. Team colors can work, but don't copy the uniform.

Shoes

Jazz shoes or white sneakers, following whatever the 2027 posting specifies.

Hair

Down, full volume, curled and set to hold through every round.

Makeup

Stage-strength and long-wear: lashes, defined brows, a bold lip. Practice the full look twice before auditions.

Audition day

You're being judged from the parking lot to the final cut. Poise, warmth and how you treat the other candidates count all day.

The bag

The day, in order

  1. Night before. Pack, lay out the look, read the toolbox once, sleep eight hours.
  2. Morning. A real breakfast, arrive early, warm up fully, then your calm-down routine.
  3. Learning choreography. Front row if allowed. Mark it small, then perform it full out.
  4. Performing. Unsure of a count? Keep smiling and keep going. Confident recovery beats freezing.
  5. Between rounds. Cheer for other candidates and stay warm. Judges look for team players.
  6. That night. Write down every combo, question and piece of feedback while it's fresh.
If it's not this year

Most of the team didn't make it the first time

Most people who make the team didn't make it on their first try. The Broncos say so themselves. Ask whether the director gives feedback, write it down, and start Module 01 again with a head start.
